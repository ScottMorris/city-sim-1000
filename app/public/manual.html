City Sim 1000 Manual

A lightweight, offline-friendly city builder. Everything runs client side and saves to your browser.

Loading the game

  • Run npm install then npm run dev to play locally.
  • Open the game in your browser. A service worker caches assets so it keeps working offline after the first load.
  • Add to your home screen or app drawer using the browser install button for a standalone feel.

Controls

  • Drag the map to pan. Use your mouse wheel or pinch to zoom.
  • Right-click to bulldoze tiles quickly — click once or hold and drag to sweep clears. Middle-click or hold Alt to pan instead. Right-click does nothing while Inspect is active.
  • Choose a tool from the toolbar and click tiles to apply it.
  • The R/C/I demand bars fill up to show approximate need for Residential, Commercial, and Industrial zones; hover one on desktop for the exact percentage.
  • Switch to the Inspect tool and click tiles to view terrain, utilities, and zone data.
  • The tool info card beside the inspector shows cost, upkeep, and stats for the active tool; hit Pin to keep it always on.
  • Use the Debug overlay button to see live stats (demand, utilities, zone counts) while tuning growth.
  • Hotkeys: Move with WASD or arrow keys, press I to enter Inspect mode, and use quick tool shortcuts: Raise (E), Lower (Q), Water paint (F), Trees (T), Road (R), Rail (L), Power Lines (P), Hydro Plant (H), Water Pump (U), Water Tower (Y), Elementary School (J), High School (N), Residential (Z), Commercial (X), Industrial (C), Park (K), Bulldoze (B), toggle the minimap (M). Set simulation speed with 1 (Slow), 2 (Fast), 3 (Ludicrous).
  • Background play: the city keeps simulating while the tab is hidden or unfocused — switch back any time and it will have carried on in real time. Long system sleeps are skipped rather than fast-forwarded.
  • Undo and redo: Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes your last action; Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z or Ctrl/Cmd+Y redoes it. A drag that paints several tiles counts as one action. Undo rewinds the city to the moment just before that action — tiles, money, population, and the clock — and redo returns you exactly to where you pressed undo. When an undo skips back a day or more, the toast says how far (e.g. "Undone — rewound 3 days"). Holding the key does not repeat: each undo takes its own keypress. Making a new change clears the redo history. Undo reaches back only as far as the current session: loading a save (or reloading the page) starts a fresh history, so a loaded city can never be damaged by undoing "past" it. Taxes and programmes are never touched by undo — only tool actions are. On phones and other compact layouts, a round ↩️ button sits above the radio in the toolbar dock for one-tap undo; it greys out when there is nothing to undo.

Touch & compact layout

  • On a phone-sized viewport (or any narrow/short window) the game switches to a compact layout automatically: a thumb-zone button at the bottom of the screen shows the current tool and its cost (e.g. "Road · $5.00") and opens a bottom sheet of every tool, grouped the same way as the desktop toolbar. Each button in the sheet shows its own cost directly — there's no hover to reveal it. Picking a tool closes the sheet.
  • One finger drives the active tool exactly like a mouse click/drag; a second finger touching down always means camera control — it cancels any in-progress paint and switches to two-finger pan and pinch-zoom instead, so a stray second finger can never leave a half-finished drag behind. Pinch keeps the point under your fingers stationary.
  • The minimap and tile inspector share one small tabbed panel (🗺️ Map / 🔍 Inspect / 🛠️ Tool) above the toolbar dock instead of floating separately. Tapping a tile with the Inspect tool active automatically switches to the Inspect tab. The Tool tab shows the active tool's full details — upkeep, footprint, output, and hints — the same information the always-on desktop tool-info card shows; tap the tab again (or Map/Inspect) to close it and get the space back for the map.
  • A few desktop hover-only details get a tap equivalent on touch: tap the 🌲 Wilderness chip for its score breakdown, tap the radio's cover thumbnail for the full track title/artist (the marquee text is hidden in compact mode to save space, but the thumbnail stays visible even without cover art, precisely so there's always something to tap), and tap a bar in the City Ledger's quarterly trend strip for its exact monthly figure. Each shows briefly as a toast — tap elsewhere to dismiss it early.
  • Right-click bulldoze and Alt-pan have no touch equivalent yet — reach Bulldoze from the tool sheet instead.

Radio

  • A compact radio widget sits at the trailing end of the toolbar (Budget/Bylaws/Settings live in the status ribbon instead). Emoji buttons control playback: ⏮️ previous, ▶️/⏸️ play or pause, ⏭️ next.
  • The current track scrolls in a short marquee with “Artist — Title”; pausing also pauses the scroll and changing tracks resets it.
  • Hover or focus the radio to reveal a small popover above the toolbar with full track details and a larger cover preview when available. If no cover is provided, the thumbnail stays hidden. On touch (where there's no hover), tap the cover thumbnail to pin the same popover open; tap it again, or tap anywhere else, to close it.
  • If no playlist ships with the build yet, the radio reads “Radio offline” and controls are disabled until audio is added.

Minimap

  • The minimap lives in the bottom-right of the HUD with a base view showing terrain, zones, roads/rail, power lines, and buildings.
  • Click or drag on the minimap to jump or slide the main camera; the current viewport stays outlined so you always know where you are.
  • Use the toggle button or hotkey M to collapse or expand the panel; size presets keep it readable without crowding the HUD.
  • Overlay modes: Base (everything), Power (powered green, unpowered red, generation/lines teal), Water (water tiles, pumps, towers, pipes in blue), Education (schools purple, served zones green, underserved amber), Wilderness (eco heatmap — lush green to urban grey), Underground (ghosts surface buildings to show pipe network and subsurface infrastructure), and Alerts (abandoned/unpowered/unhappy zones glow warm). The same mode tints the main map so you can spot issues without relying on the minimap alone.

Tools

  • Terraform: convert terrain back to land and flatten rough edges.
  • Water: flood tiles to create lakes or rivers.
  • Water Services: water is opt-in — until you place your first pump, water tower, or pipe, buildings don't require it and run happily on power alone. Placing any water infrastructure switches the requirement on city-wide, so plan pipe coverage as you expand. Open the submenu for utility buildings. Pipes are the default selection; place Pump for basic supply, Water Tower for extra capacity that requires power and a pipe/road connection, or lay Pipes to connect water sources to your city. Selecting Pipes automatically switches to the Underground view, where pumps and towers show pipe attachment points.
  • Education: open the Schools submenu and place Elementary or High School. They require power, use the road network for an 8–9 tile reach, and their coverage overlay shows which zones they serve; underserved neighbourhoods see a small demand and decay penalty.
  • Trees: add greenery for happiness and decoration.
  • Road / Rail: lay transport networks for citizens and freight.
  • Power Line: connect neighbourhoods to your hydro plants; can run over roads/rail without breaking access.
  • Hydro Plant: produces clean electricity. Place near water for flavour.
  • Residential / Commercial / Industrial: zone your city to grow population, shops, and factories. Zones cannot overwrite roads or rail—bulldoze first.
  • Bulldozer: remove buildings or networks without affecting terrain.

Bylaws

  • Open the Bylaws modal from the status ribbon (next to Budget/Settings) to set a city-wide lighting standard.
  • Mixed corridors are the neutral baseline. Energy-efficient lighting trims civic/zone power draw and upkeep with a slight mood dip. Carbon-arc lamps boost ambience with higher power use and upkeep.
  • The modal shows projected power demand and monthly upkeep deltas compared to your current policy; toggles apply immediately and save with your city.
  • Wilderness programmes live here too: Nature Reserve (unlocks at Wilderness 60) strengthens patch bonuses and softens fragmentation penalties for a flat monthly cost, and Green Industry subsidizes clean industry so industrial tiles weigh far less on the wilderness score, priced per industrial zone.
  • District overlays are planned; today the chosen lighting bylaw applies everywhere.

Economy & Utilities

  • The Budget panel shows your cash plus a monthly net projection: green for a surplus, red for a deficit, neutral when flat.
  • Months are 30 days long; the HUD shows the current month and day so the monthly budget numbers line up with the calendar.
  • Open the City Ledger (📊 in the status ribbon, or click the treasury chip) to see where money comes from and where it goes: two flow bars on a shared scale, revenue and expense breakdowns with per-type detail (coal vs wind plants, parks vs schools, and so on), a quarterly trend, a runway estimate, and an Advisor strip when the Narrative layer is enabled.
  • The Ledger's City Hall sliders set tax rates (0–20%, neutral 9%) per zone class and department funding (0–100%) for transport, power, and civic services. Changes apply immediately. Taxes above 9% raise cash but cool that class's demand. Underfunding saves upkeep but has consequences: low transport funding frustrates commuters (demand drag on everything), low power funding browns out your plants, and low civic funding crowds the schools.
  • Power is generated by plants; roads and rail also conduct power in addition to power lines. Water is pumped from sources; roads and rail also conduct water, and you can lay Pipes underground to span gaps.
  • Buildings without water will stop working and show a water-drop icon. Their usage drops to zero, which can stop the "Deficit" alert, so check your pipes if buildings go inactive.
  • Schools draw power and add upkeep; their reach rides the road network. Underserved areas see a modest drop in Residential demand and a higher chance of decay, and weak high-school coverage trims Commercial/Industrial demand.
  • Growth needs power/water ≥ 0 and a road chain; zones adjacent to roads can power neighbours through the network.
  • When power or water drops below zero you'll see a sticky warning toast; it clears with a follow-up notice once supply returns.
  • Starter demand is seeded when the city is empty so pre-painted zones can begin to grow; normal demand formulas resume after the first builds.
  • Unemployment and job vacancies are aggregate signals: vacancies nudge Residential demand up while unemployment nudges Commercial/Industrial up. They’re gentle modifiers, not individual citizens.
  • Zones can abandon if demand stays very low, power is missing, or tiles are unhappy. Abandoned lots auto-clear back to zoned tiles and will regrow when demand recovers.
  • Over-zoning is soft: pending zones gently trim demand with caps and a small floor so big paint jobs trickle-build instead of stalling. Strong demand will still punch through, but power deficits can drive it to zero.
  • Demand bars show where citizens want you to grow next.

Wilderness

  • The 🌲 Wilderness chip in the status ribbon scores how much of your map is thriving nature, 0–100, with a trend arrow. Hover it (or tap it, on touch) for the biggest contributors, positive and negative.
  • Nature is worth more as an ecosystem: contiguous forest and park patches beat scattered trees, waterfront greenery earns a wetland bonus, and isolated nature tiles are penalized for fragmentation.
  • The minimap's Wilderness overlay paints the eco field on the map: lush green where nature thrives, grey where urban pressure dominates.
  • Industry, roads, rail, and power infrastructure push the score down — coal plants hardest of all; wind and solar tread far more lightly, and hydro sits in between.
  • A green city attracts residents (higher Residential demand) and keeps zones happier; a paved one does the opposite.
  • Keep the score above 60 and a Tourism dividend appears in the City Ledger, scaling with your population and how far the score climbs.
  • Hitting 60 also unlocks the Nature Reserve programme in Bylaws; Green Industry is available any time. Both cost money and show up as a Wilderness programmes line in the Ledger.

News ticker

  • A ticker bar in the HUD cycles short, grounded updates about city conditions.
  • Most items refresh at month end; critical alerts can appear immediately and stay until resolved.
  • Disable the Narrative layer or the ticker itself from Settings if you prefer a quieter HUD.

Temporary caveats

  • Power lines can overlay roads/rail; future sprites will reflect crossings.
  • Zoning over transport is blocked to prevent accidental overwrite; bulldoze transport first.

Saving & backups

  • Open the 💾 menu in the top status ribbon for all save actions.
  • Use Save to store your city in the browser and Load to restore it. Saves capture the simulation's exact state — every building, the clock, the treasury (down to the cent), even the dice the city will roll next — so a loaded city carries on precisely where it left off.
  • Download exports a .citysim file so you can back your city up or move it between devices.
  • Upload restores any exported city — including JSON saves downloaded before the .citysim format existed; old browser saves are upgraded automatically the first time you play.
  • Loading a save starts a fresh undo history — the loaded city is the floor that undo can never damage.
  • Autosave: the game quietly saves to its own slot every minute while you play, and whenever the tab is hidden or closed. If a crash or accidental refresh strikes, the game restores whichever save is newest and tells you (e.g. "Restored autosave from 2 min ago"). Your manual Save slot is never overwritten by autosave.

The status ribbon

  • The bar across the top shows treasury and monthly net, power and water balances, R/C/I demand columns, the calendar, population/jobs, and the 🌲 Wilderness score.
  • Hover any chip for details (the treasury and Wilderness chips are also tap targets, on touch — treasury opens the City Ledger, Wilderness shows its breakdown); the R/C/I columns rise with zone demand, SimCity-style.
  • Right side: a speed menu (icon shows the active tier — 🐢 Slow, 🐇 Fast, ⚡ Ludicrous — hotkeys 1/2/3), a ⏸ pause toggle (hotkey Space; picking a speed while paused resumes at that speed), 📊 budget, 📜 bylaws, the 💾 saves menu, 📖 this manual, ⚙️ settings, and 🛠️ debug tools.
